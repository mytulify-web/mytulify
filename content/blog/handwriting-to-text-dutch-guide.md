[Dutch Handwriting to Text](/handwriting-ocr/handwriting-to-text-dutch) is the tool for one job: convert Dutch handwriting from a photo into editable Dutch text, with optional translation into another language. This guide covers the inputs to gather, one pass you can check, and the point where the result stops being enough.

## What to bring to the Dutch Handwriting to Text

Use a sharp photo where the Dutch writing fills the frame. Crop the desk, the fingers, and the glare. One page at a time is more reliable than a whole notebook in one shot.

Dutch Handwriting to Text sends the input to a server so the work can run. Do not include passwords or account numbers. The free plan has a daily limit.

## A worked pass with the Dutch Handwriting to Text

Upload one page you can still read yourself. Keep the original Dutch text, then turn on translation only if you need a second language. Dutch handwriting often joins ij and uses a long loop on the g and y. Read names twice; a capital can look like a lower-case letter.

Do that pass with a case you already understand. Then replace it with the real case. Changing one input at a time is how you see which field actually moved the result.

## How to check the result

Put the photo and the text side by side. Check names, numbers, and any word you would quote. A gap or a [illegible] mark is a place to look again, not a word to invent.

## Where the Dutch Handwriting to Text stops

Dutch Handwriting to Text reads the handwriting in the photo. It does not verify that the note is true, and a translation can smooth over a word the model was unsure about.

If your decision depends on a rule this page does not mention, a lender, a clinic, a teacher, a client, or the vendor of the file, use their rule. Keep this result as the draft you compare against that rule.
