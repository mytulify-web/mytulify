[Scientific Calculator](/calculators/scientific-calculator) is the tool for one job: full-function calculator with trig, logs, exponents, and constants. This guide covers the inputs to gather, one pass you can check, and the point where the result stops being enough.

## What to bring to the Scientific Calculator

Use the degree or radian mode that matches the problem. Sine of 90 degrees is 1. Sine of 90 radians is not.

Scientific Calculator runs in your browser. The file or the figures stay on your device.

## A worked pass with the Scientific Calculator

Type a problem you know, such as the square root of 144, or 2 raised to the 10th, before you trust a longer expression. Parentheses change the order of operations.

Do that pass with a case you already understand. Then replace it with the real case. Changing one input at a time is how you see which field actually moved the result.

## How to check the result

Watch the mode indicator. A wrong angle mode is the most common reason a correct-looking expression is wrong.

## Where the Scientific Calculator stops

It calculates what you type. It does not know which formula the homework asked for.

If your decision depends on a rule this page does not mention, a lender, a clinic, a teacher, a client, or the vendor of the file, use their rule. Keep this result as the draft you compare against that rule.
