[Japanese Handwriting to Text](/handwriting-ocr/handwriting-to-text-japanese) is the tool for one job: convert Japanese handwriting from a photo into editable Japanese text, with optional translation into another language. This guide covers the inputs to gather, one pass you can check, and the point where the result stops being enough.

## What to bring to the Japanese Handwriting to Text

Use a sharp photo where the Japanese writing fills the frame. Crop the desk, the fingers, and the glare. One page at a time is more reliable than a whole notebook in one shot.

Japanese Handwriting to Text sends the input to a server so the work can run. Do not include passwords or account numbers. The free plan has a daily limit.

## A worked pass with the Japanese Handwriting to Text

Upload one page you can still read yourself. Keep the original Japanese text, then turn on translation only if you need a second language. Japanese mixes kanji, hiragana, and katakana. A kanji guess that is only close is still the wrong word. Keep the original script beside the translation.

Do that pass with a case you already understand. Then replace it with the real case. Changing one input at a time is how you see which field actually moved the result.

## How to check the result

Put the photo and the text side by side. Check names, numbers, and any word you would quote. A gap or a [illegible] mark is a place to look again, not a word to invent.

## Where the Japanese Handwriting to Text stops

Japanese Handwriting to Text reads the handwriting in the photo. It does not verify that the note is true, and a translation can smooth over a word the model was unsure about.

If your decision depends on a rule this page does not mention, a lender, a clinic, a teacher, a client, or the vendor of the file, use their rule. Keep this result as the draft you compare against that rule.
