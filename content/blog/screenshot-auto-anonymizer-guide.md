[Screenshot Auto-Anonymizer](/image-tools/screenshot-auto-anonymizer) is the tool for one job: blur or blackout faces, text, credit cards, and sensitive objects in screenshots — 100% private in your browser. This guide covers the inputs to gather, one pass you can check, and the point where the result stops being enough.

## What to bring to the Screenshot Auto-Anonymizer

Use the original image. Blur or blackout faces, text, credit cards, and sensitive objects in screenshots — 100% private in your browser.

Screenshot Auto-Anonymizer runs in your browser. The file or the figures stay on your device.

## A worked pass with the Screenshot Auto-Anonymizer

The blur or blackout is burned into the download. Faces, text, cards, and the other detectors can miss something. Look at the result before you send it. The original file stays on your device.

Do that pass with a case you already understand. Then replace it with the real case. Changing one input at a time is how you see which field actually moved the result.

## How to check the result

Compare the download with the original at 100% zoom on the part you care about: a face, a line of text, or the edge of the crop.

## Where the Screenshot Auto-Anonymizer stops

Screenshot Auto-Anonymizer changes the copy you download. It does not edit the file still sitting in your folder until you replace it.

If your decision depends on a rule this page does not mention, a lender, a clinic, a teacher, a client, or the vendor of the file, use their rule. Keep this result as the draft you compare against that rule.
